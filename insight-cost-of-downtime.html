Cost of downtime

Every hour of downtime costs more than a year of preparedness

Preparedness is not an insurance premium. It is the cheaper side of the ledger.

Every hour of downtime costs an organization on three fronts at once: revenue that never returns, regulatory exposure in sectors with strict mandates for documented recovery, and reputational damage that takes years — and marketing spend — to rebuild.

For critical systems, that cost is quantified in hundreds of thousands per hour, before counting fines and lost trust. Set against that, the cost of building and testing a recovery plan is small and predictable — a known, budgeted expense instead of an open-ended one.

Most companies hope their IT will recover. Hope is not a strategy, and auditors, regulators and boards know the difference between a plan and an assumption. The difference is evidence: recovery that is tested, documented and monitored, with verified restores and audit-ready reporting behind it.
